Membership System

A five-level development pathway based on the responsibility a member can reliably take on.

From participation to multiplying capability

Members develop from taking initiative and contributing constructively to independently executing work, leading projects, and making other people and initiatives more effective.

Membership is separate from the Core Team

A membership level reflects a student's demonstrated capability and responsibility. A Core Team post is a formal organizational position. A student may hold both at the same time; one does not automatically grant the other.

L1 Defining characteristic · Initiative

Community Member

“Can you take initiative and participate constructively?”

Capabilities

  • Identify problems
  • Submit problems
  • Initiate a project
  • Join open projects
  • Recruit collaborators
  • Participate in workshops
  • Experiment with ideas
  • Access community learning resources
Club expectations
CuriosityWillingness to learn Respect for community norms Basic accountability for commitments
What the club gains

A larger problem-discovery network, more ideas and student perspectives, more potential project participants and future leaders, and greater campus reach.

Return at this level: potential and participation
L2 Defining characteristic · Reliability

Contributor

“Can other people rely on you?”

Capabilities

  • Complete assigned tasks
  • Collaborate effectively
  • Conduct research
  • Collect evidence
  • Take responsibility for deliverables
  • Give constructive feedback
  • Document work
  • Communicate progress
What the club gains

Reliable help with research, student feedback, solution testing, project documentation, assigned tasks, events, peer support and domain knowledge. L2 members become the club's distributed workforce instead of the Core Team doing everything.

Return at this level: reliable contribution
L3 Defining characteristic · Autonomy

Independent Practitioner

“Can you independently take responsibility for a meaningful piece of work?”

Capabilities

  • Decompose problems
  • Plan execution
  • Select appropriate methods and tools
  • Conduct independent research
  • Build and test solutions
  • Handle unexpected obstacles
  • Evaluate own results
  • Guide less-experienced collaborators
What the club gains

Reduced supervision costs and greater execution capacity without a proportional increase in leadership overhead.

Return at this level: autonomous execution
L4 Defining characteristic · Ownership

Project Steward

“Can you take responsibility for an entire project and its team?”

Capabilities

  • Define project objectives
  • Establish execution plans
  • Coordinate multidisciplinary teams
  • Delegate responsibilities
  • Manage resources and timelines
  • Resolve team conflicts
  • Communicate with stakeholders
  • Ensure testing and outcome measurement
What the club gains

More projects running simultaneously, reduced Core Team workload, accountable project owners, mentorship for younger members, and fewer projects collapsing from lack of coordination.

Return at this level: delegated project leadership
L5 Defining characteristic · Multiplying others' capabilities

Community Steward

“Can you make other people and multiple initiatives more effective?”

Capabilities

  • Coordinate multiple projects or initiatives
  • Mentor Project Stewards
  • Develop new contributors and leaders
  • Identify recurring problems across projects
  • Improve collaboration between teams
  • Resolve complex organizational issues
  • Build external relationships
  • Develop community-wide initiatives
What the club gains · Multiplication of organizational capacity

New leaders are developed, recurring problems are identified, internal processes improve, different teams become better connected, and partnerships are managed more effectively.

Return at this level: multiplied community capacity
Important distinction: these levels describe a member's demonstrated capacity and the scope of responsibility they can take on. They are not committee posts or an alternative Core Team hierarchy. Core Team members can also progress through or hold a level in this membership system.

How the structure works

Authority should decrease downward while execution capacity increases.
